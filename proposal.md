# Policy Proposal: AI and the Labour Market

## Prepared For
Deputy Secretary, Employment & Skills  
Department of Employment and Workplace Relations

## Executive Summary

## 1. Problem Definition & Context

It is impossible to ignore how AI is quickly taking over workplace tasks and redefining jobs in the job market. Improvements in AI give firms the option to automate repetitive and non qualitative work that can be done more efficiently, in lesser time, and cost less than paying a real person. AI has the potential to improve productivity but risks opening risks to inequality and labour displacement. AI is expected to effect jobs unevenly because of its robotic nature. This means it may negatively effect clerical type work while positively impacting high skilled work. This would raise concerns about the pay gap or job vacancy disparity between these 2 roles causing a large pay gap. 

Although AI is not expected to eliminate jobs in the near future, it is expected to reshape the tasks for different jobs in the industrial sector. The government now has an important job of creating policies that will allow the benefits of new technologies to be shared evenly and not just to one beneficial group.

## 2. Analysis of Impacts

### Employment

First of all. Clerical, data entry, and administration jobs can be completely taken over or at least redefined in the short term. This is because of being able to complete these tasks faster and at lower costs. However like with every new technology this creates space for new jobs to emerge or at least increase the number of certain jobs already existing. Also, a third effect could be the re-definement of job roles meaning the job has not been taken by AI but the tasks involved with the job are now different. Transition periods are also very important and I believe this is where the policies need to be focused on because of how volatile and crucial any type of transition is for the economy. 

### Tasks

Key distinction when evaluating the effects AI has on jobs is Automation and Augmentation. Most basic job roles will be automated by AI meaning the role will be filled by it while Augmentation means that the AI will complete or assist in tasks that would normally take a long time to complete which helps complete the job quicker. So that means high skilled roles will not be automated by AI but will change the tasks involved with a specific job.

### Wages

Just like the industrial revolution or other technological advances, we may not see equal distribution of wage increase or benefits for an uncertain amount of time. In fact wage increases may not be distributed equally. First large tech companies like Meta, Microsoft, or NVidia have a monopoly when it comes to AI investment which has the possibility to constrict the job availability of these roles. Another thing is that this is a small section of the economy meaning people without the skills in AI development and utilization can lose their jobs or will be unable to find new work. This can cause increased gap between skilled and non skilled as well as stagnation in non skilled job wages.

### Industrial Structure

The industrial sector is the same concept as the wages except this time it involves the growing inequality of established big companies compared to smaller tech companies. The main concern would be the reduction in competition due to the monopolization of the AI sector with the biggest companies being able to invest the most into AI and hire majority of the skilled workers.

### Distributional & Equity Effects

The final sector is which social sectors are affected by this transition. Firstly there is no doubt just like in anything, that opportunities are unequal. There will be vulnerable workers like those who will be replaced or not given training on AI. Next is access to this type of education or training especially in regional areas. Overall the people affected can be spilt in 2 categories, which is people who will be replaced or have no opportunities to be educated, and trained in AI. And, the people who have the skills, means of training, or people who are already in established high skilled non automotive job roles.

## 3. Policy Options

### Option 1
This policy is Re-Skilling Initiative to help current workers transition better into their AI assisted roles. AI skills are now increasingly desired in candidates. With this training initiative current and potential employees would be equipped with the skills needed to complete their new tasks. This training could be short online or university courses, or employer supported training. This would reduce unemployment rates and allow for employee adaptability and longevity.

### Option 2
The second is a Transition Assistance Policy. This would be similar to stimulus checks during Covid where people affected would receive compensation for the transition until they are able to establish themselves back into their industry. This would increase the initiative to still spend or keep money moving in the economy which is what the stimulus checks of the pandemic tried to do. It also allows for displaced employees to find work again and avoids financial stagnation of an entire group of people.

### Option 3
AI Worker Protection Policy would require companies to always disclose when AI is being used in any of the companies operations. This can be things like basic tasks, recording, performance evaluations, as well as hiring and firing. This ensures that AI is being used responsibly in the daily operations.
